The project

Who is hospitalised after road crashes in Australia, how has that changed from 2011 to 2021, and who is hurt most seriously? Built for the general public and road safety policymakers.

Colour choices

  • Dark burgundy as a neutral data backdrop: #160C12 and #25151D frame the data without encoding injury severity. Rose (#F28AA5) is reserved for decorative accents and focus cues.
  • One distinguishable hue per measure: Okabe–Ito sky blue (#56B4E9) = hospitalisation cases; orange (#E69F00) = total bed days; bluish green (#009E73) = bed days per case. Colours encode measure identity, not risk or severity.
  • Measured contrast: relative to the #25151D panel, blue = 7.56:1, orange = 7.75:1, green = 5.10:1, main text = 15.65:1, and secondary text = 9.77:1 (WCAG relative-luminance formula). These opaque combinations exceed 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for meaningful graphic marks, but do not prove that all controls and interactions pass an accessibility audit.
  • Data-type-appropriate palettes: the donut uses qualitative hues for five nominal road-user categories and neutral grey for the grouped remainder, rather than a misleading light-to-dark ranking gradient.
  • Redundant encoding: rises and falls have a zero baseline and signed percentages; the comparative line chart uses a solid cases line and dashed bed-days line. Labels, tooltips and legends reinforce colour.
  • Design basis: Okabe & Ito, Color Universal Design (https://jfly.uni-koeln.de/color/); ColorBrewer 2.0 (https://colorbrewer2.org/); W3C WCAG 2.2 (https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/). See the Design Book for complete APA 7 citations.

Font choices

  • Chakra Petch for headings and big numbers: squared, technical letters fit the futuristic brief and stay legible when large.
  • IBM Plex Sans for everything else: designed for small sizes, with clearly different numerals, which matters in number-heavy charts.

Layout and chart choices

  • One screen per page: a dashboard should be seen at a glance, so detail lives on separate pages.
  • Left to right is the story: how has it changed, who is hit hardest, who is hurt most seriously.
  • Line for change over time. Bars for ranking categories. Bubbles for three variables at once. Mirrored bars for comparing two groups. Donut for share of the total.
  • Linked charts: choosing a road user highlights it everywhere, so the same group can be seen from several angles.
  • Honest axes: bar charts start at zero. The line charts do not, and say so on screen.
